1. Core Axiom: Volatility is Risk, Not Direction

One of the most persistent rookie mistakes in cryptocurrency and volatile asset trading is equating high volatility with bullish momentum. Surging volatility does not imply upward direction; it represents expanded price dispersion, wider tails, severe slippage, and increased liquidation risk.

Volatility Ratio Formulation: R_vol = σ_forecast / σ_baseline_72h

When R_vol ≥ 1.80, an emergency REDUCE signal triggers automatically, forcing immediate capital de-risking regardless of directional probability.

2. Dynamic ATR Position Sizing & The 2% Capital Risk Rule

Never risk more than 2.0% of total portfolio equity on a single trade. Stop-loss distances must never be arbitrary fixed percentages; they must adapt dynamically to the asset's current Average True Range (ATR):

Dynamic ATR Stop Distance: Stop_Distance = max(2.0 × ATR_14, 0.006 × Price) Position_Size = min((Capital × 0.02) / Stop_Distance, (Capital × 0.95) / Price)

In calm regimes, the stop distance contracts, allowing larger position size. In volatile expansions, the stop distance widens, automatically scaling down position size to protect capital.

3. Asymmetric 2:1 Minimum Reward-to-Risk (2R)

A trading system with a 40% win rate can remain highly profitable if winners consistently outsize losers. The platform enforces a strict 2R minimum profit target:

Target Price Equation: Take_Profit = Entry_Price ± (2.0 × Stop_Distance)

If the distance to nearest major resistance does not allow for a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio, the setup is rejected with a HOLD recommendation.

4. Volatility Clustering & Mandelbrot Regime Shifts

Financial asset prices do not follow independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) Gaussian normal distributions. As Benoit Mandelbrot and Robert Engle demonstrated, volatility exhibits clustering: large changes tend to be followed by large changes, of either sign.

Rule of Clustering: After a volatility shock erupts, expect subsequent shocks for at least 24 to 72 hours. Do not attempt mean-reversion counter-trend trades during volatility expansion.

5. 5-Factor Model Concordance Scoring

A statistical edge requires alignment across independent, orthogonal models. High-conviction signals (STRONG BUY / STRONG SELL) require at least 4 out of 5 factors (80%) in agreement:

  1. GARCH Econometric Variance: Conditional volatility remains stationary (≤ 1.40× historical median).
  2. Deep LSTM Recurrence: Sequential hidden states point in trade direction (≥ 52% or ≤ 48%).
  3. Transformer Self-Attention: 48-hour multi-head attention weights confirm momentum persistence.
  4. Technical Trend: Moving average alignment (Price > EMA 20 > EMA 50 for longs).
  5. Macro Polarity: Fear & Greed index and financial RSS headline sentiment concordant.

6. Hard Portfolio Drawdown Circuit Breakers

If peak-to-trough portfolio equity drawdown reaches 20.0%, the risk manager trips the emergency circuit breaker:

Emergency Circuit Breaker: All open positions are liquidated, new orders are blocked, and trading is halted until the operator completes an explicit post-mortem audit.

7. Cognitive Biases & Common Trader Mistakes

Revenge Trading

Increasing position size immediately after a loss to "get even". Solution: Mandate minimum 1-hour cooling off period.

Moving Stop Losses

Widening a stop loss when the market approaches it. Solution: Stop losses are set automatically and cannot be moved adversely.

Overtrading in Churn Regimes

Executing trades in low-conviction range-bound chop. Solution: The HOLD regime preserves capital and eliminates fee bleed.

Ignoring Event Risk

Holding oversized leveraged positions into high-impact CPI, FOMC, or ETF rate decisions. Solution: Event risk dampening automatically trims exposure.
